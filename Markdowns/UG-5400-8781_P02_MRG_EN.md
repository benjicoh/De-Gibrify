# CARTOMERGE™ Image Integration Module

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/CartoMergePlus\_intro\_c3-6\_EN.xml

With the **CARTOMERGE™ Image Integration Module** (CARTOMERGE™ Module), you can import segmented CT or MRI image studies into the CARTO™ 3 System. Three-dimensional (3D) surface images of the cardiac chamber or structure are automatically created when the image study is imported. After you register the surface images to a CARTO™ 3 Map, you can use the surface images to guide mapping in a particular area of interest. [[1]](#footnote-1)

Failure to do so might result in incorrect placement of the catheter. |

## Importing Images

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/CartoMerge\_UG\_importReformatted\_EN.xml

You can import CT or MRI studies to the CARTO™ 3 System via CD or network connection. Studies can be original (raw data sent in 2D slices and 3D consistent) or processed on a radiology workstation, as long as they are 3D-consistent and saved in DICOM 3 format. Derived or reformatted series **must not** include a localizer slice.

If the imported images are not segmented, use the Image Processing Package or one of the CARTOSEG™ Segmentation Modules[[2]](#footnote-2) to import, render, segment, and edit the scans.

The CARTOMERGE™ Module supports use of derived or imported series from the General Electric AW Workstation, Siemens Leonardo Workstation, and Philips Extended Brilliance Workspace (EBW). It also supports import of 3D models (single segmented structure) created on the CardEP Module of the AW Workstation. Importable data is defined as follows:

* **Original 3D Consistent (raw data):** An original volume of data provided in bulk as 2D images which compose a 3D consistent volume, typically provided from a CT or MRI scanner.
* **Derived/Reformatted 3D Consistent:** The same as raw data, except that the volume is derived on the radiology workstation. The voxels of the segmented volume retain their original values, while other voxels receive an intensity value of zero (0). Data is saved in standard DICOM 3 format and should be 3D consistent.
* **3D Model:** A 3D model is reformatted on the CardEP module of the AW workstation. The voxels of the segmented volume retain their original values, while other voxels receive an intensity value of zero (0). Data is saved in a proprietary Biosense Webster format supported by major CT/MRI system vendors such as Siemens, Philips and GE.

When importing either a 3D model or reformatted series, the surface of interest is automatically extracted. This latter capability enables direct import to the CARTO™ 3 System directly via the Import Images command. The imported data is used to create 3D segmented surface images.

When a study is imported, each valid series is saved to the system hard disk as a unique 3D Image Study. The name of the imported study is based on the numbers of the original image study and series. For example, Image Study 6, series1 is called "Study6.1" in the image integration module. All relevant DICOM attributes of the study are also imported.

### Registration Tools Toolbar

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/imageIntegration\_toolbar\_C3\_EN.xml

The Registration Tools toolbar provides quick access to the following options (in this order):

* Visual Alignment: Moves the volume image and map such that the both landmarks of the pair occupy the exact same location.
* Landmark Registration: Registers all surface images to all acquired CARTO™ 3 Maps, based on the applied landmark pairs. At least three landmarks must be applied for this option to be enabled.
* Surface Registration: Registers one or more surface images to matching CARTO™ 3 Maps based on acquired points in the map. This option is available after performing landmark registration or visual alignment.
* Undo: Undoes the previous position and orientation change to the surface image (via Rotate, X-Y Move or application of a registration option). You can consecutively click Undo.
* Redo: Reapplies the previous action that was undone.

A *landmark* is a named flag that is attached to a specific site on a surface image, contour, or a point on a map ([Figure 3: "Landmarks"](#target9000002)). For best results, landmarks should be placed as far apart as possible within the target registration area.

A *landmark pair* is a set of two identical flags (name and color) that are attached to both a surface image and a point on the map or a contour. Landmark pairs are used to register a surface image to a CARTO™ 3 Map. Thus, they must be placed in the same position on the surface image and on the map. For example, if a landmark is placed in the coronary sinus, it should be *attached* to a point acquired in the same part of the coronary sinus. When the landmark is attached to the corresponding point, a landmark pair is created.

Landmarks can be renamed or deleted. Landmark pairs can also be detached.

The landmark flags are broken when they are not attached to another landmark. Landmark pairs are two identical flags; one is applied to the volume image and one is applied to a point on a map (or a contour on the map). The Point shortcut menu is used to attach the landmark on the surface image to the point on the map.

## Verifying Registration

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/VerifyingRegistration\_c3\_EN.xml

Verification of effective registration is essential, to be able to refer to the surface image during a CARTO™ 3 Study. Statistical data and calculations regarding the closeness of fit are viewed in the Landmark and Surface tabs of the Match Statistics panel. A visual representation of the fit can also be displayed via the Registration Match View option. Careful review of the information provided via these options will help determine how closely the surface image and CARTO™ 3 Map match after registration.

## Segmentation of Image Studies

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/Understanding Segmentation of Image Studies\_EN.xml

Much of the terminology used in referring to the data of CT or MRI image scans comes from its long history. Originally, all scans were axial images acquired by a lengthy sequential scanning procedure. Modern technology enables quicker acquisition of this data and, for MRI, acquisition along dissimilar axes.

When a raw series or image study is imported into the Image Processing Package, all two-dimensional (2D) slices are rendered into a single three-dimensional (3D) volume. Segmentation is required for separating this volume into the *structure(s) of interest* (SOI). In most cases, the SOI is the area of most interest for real-time mapping, such as the left atrium or left ventricle.

The gray shades in the scan are representative of different densities or intensities. Imaging software helps users set the range (termed "window") of densities (from out of 4,096 possible values), to be displayed in different shades of gray, and adjust the center of that window (termed "level").

Color is applied to those areas of the 2D slices that match the desired threshold intensity; these colored areas are termed *Labels*. After the volume has been segmented into SOIs, labels are colored to match their corresponding segmented object, for easy identification in the 2D slices.

## Overview of the Segmentation Process

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/Overview of the Segmentation Process\_EN.xml

The segmentation process involves loading an image study, setting the optimal intensity value, and selecting the labeled *volume of interest* (VOI).

This VOI becomes the baseline for segmenting the rest of the image study. This is done by applying a special algorithm in the CARTOMERGE™ Image Processing Package. The algorithm takes all voxels (volume unit of the CT or MRI) within the same intensity threshold ***and*** adjacent to the selection, to provide a 3D volume based only on that data.

At this point, there is a single segmented volume. The surface volume may now be exported. However, when working with a data series, it is likely that multiple SOI segmenting will be required.

Segmentation to various structures is performed by applying *seeds* to all areas of the volume with which you want to work or remove from the final display. For best results, seeds are placed in the middle of each structure of interest. When you apply the Segment command, a second algorithm is implemented from each seed to their respective borders (that is, implementation of the algorithm ceases when the borders meet). The result is separate anatomical regions of interest, or SOIs.

This process of applying seeds and segmenting might have to be repeated until you are satisfied with the quality of the segmented 3D image study. If automatic segmentation is inadequate (due to poor study image quality, artifacts, etc.), you can manually edit the volume in 2D or 3D mode.

It is important to verify the results of your segmentation. Using the Slice Tools, scroll through the study and review the borders of each segmented object against the original 2D data slice.

In order to use the segmented volume during a CARTO™ 3 Study, the volume must be extracted to a surface image. This is accomplished by exporting it to the CARTO™ 3 System. After exporting, all volume data is removed, providing you with an anatomical shape that can be registered to the mapping space of the CARTO™ 3 System, via the CARTOMERGE™ Image Integration Module.

### Intensity Threshold Labels

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/Understanding the Intensity Threshold of Labels\_EN.xml

In order to segment an SOI, the structure must be marked on the image. This is done by applying color (termed *labels*) to the image. The label must cover the image as homogeneously as possible, and define the border clearly, to assure quality segmentation.

The dark green bar in the Threshold Settings dialog box indicates the range of densities that will be labeled (i.e., given color). Moving or stretching this bar changes the range.
