# CARTOSEG™ CT Segmentation Module

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_ct\_intro\_v6\_EN.xml

The **CARTOSEG™ CT Segmentation Module** is an advanced segmentation tool that generates segmentations of various anatomical structures from CT scans of the heart.[[1]](#footnote-1)

With the CARTOSEG™ CT Segmentation Module, you can import, render, segment, and edit CT scans for real-time use during CARTO™ 3 System studies with the CARTOMERGE™ Module. The CARTOSEG™ CT Segmentation Module provides several semi-automatic segmentation options and multiple editing options.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ![DxNoteGeneric.png](data:image/png;base64...) | NOTE  Imported images represent the structure at the time of acquisition, and do not reflect changes since that time. |

When a scan is imported into the CARTOSEG™ CT Segmentation Module, all two-dimensional (2D) slices are rendered into a single three-dimensional (3D) volume. Segmentation is required to separate this volume into the structure(s) of interest (SOI). In most cases, the SOI is the area of most interest for real-time mapping, such as the left atrium or left ventricle.

The gray shades in the scan represent different densities or intensities. Segmentation to SOIs is based on the selected intensities and a cardiac model.

Colors are applied to the various SOIs. These colored areas are called *labels*. The same labels apply to both the 3D and 2D views, for easy identification of the segmented SOIs.

## General Workflow

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_ct\_seg\_v6\_EN.xml

The standard workflow for CT scans consists of the following stages:

* Loading scans for segmentation. Up to 5 CT scans can be loaded.
* Previewing the loaded scans.
* Applying semi-automatic and/or manual segmentation.
* Reviewing and editing the segmented images.
* Approve and save the resulting images to be used during a CARTO™ 3 Study.

Refer to the *CARTOMERGE™ User Guide* for details on performing a study in the CARTO™ 3 System.

# Safety Information

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/CARTOSEG\_CT\_SafetyInformation\_EN.xml

All safety considerations, cautions, and warnings that apply to the general use of the CARTO™ 3 System also apply while using this module. In addition, the following considerations apply:

* The responsibility for verifying segmentation quality rests with the physician as the licensed medical practitioner. Verify quality throughout the process of segmentation. If necessary, all segmentation can be cancelled and restarted.
* Use of the Match Statistics and Registration Match View does not replace your common clinical practice for real-time verification of catheter locations throughout the procedure (inspection of IC signals, direct imaging guidance such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, etc.). Final determination of effective registration is mandatory, and should be performed on the target area of interest.
* Imported images represent the structure at the time of acquisition, and do not reflect changes since that time.
* When using the segmented images during a study with the CARTOMERGE™ Module, use your common clinical practice for real-time verification of the locations of catheters and adjacent anatomical structures throughout the procedure (inspection of IC signals, direct imaging guidance such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, etc.). Consider the respiratory effect on the location of anatomical structures. Failure to verify real-time locations might result in incorrect placement of the catheter.
* During a study with the CARTOMERGE™ Module:
  + Displaying the esophagus segmentation does not replace your common clinical practice for avoiding esophageal injury and fistula during ablation.
  + Displaying the pulmonary vein segmentation does not replace your common clinical practice for verifying the real-time location and avoiding pulmonary vein injury and stenosis during ablation.
  + Displaying the coronary sinus and coronary artery segmentation does not replace your common clinical practice for verifying the real-time location and avoiding injury during ablation.

# Applying Segmentation

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_ct\_ApplyingSeg\_v6\_EN.xml

To continue to the segmentation stage, click the Segmentation button.

The Segmentation stage becomes active. The segmentation toolbar appears, with the following buttons: semi-automatic, manual, coronary sinus, coronary arteries, and esophagus.

![cartoseg_segmentation_buttons.png](data:image/png;base64...)

The module performs semi-automatic segmentation of the heart chambers based on the typical tissue features of the various anatomical structures. This segmentation process produces the four heart chambers and the aorta. Segmentation of the coronary sinus, coronary arteries, and esophagus can be done using the additional semi-automatic segmentation tools.

Alternatively, you can use manual segmentation to segment the anatomical structure. To perform manual segmentation, you must specify the range of intensities that define the particular structure.

You can apply various editing options to the segmented images.

## Manual CT Segmentation

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_ct\_manSeg\_v6-4\_EN.xml

With the Manual Segmentation tool, you can segment a new object based on a selected range of intensities.

1. Click the Manual Segmentation button.
2. Click the area of interest in one of the 2D views. An initial global segmentation is created based on the intensities of the selected area.

When the process is completed, the segmented result is displayed in the 3D view with the clipping plane, and the intensity threshold bar appears. The new segmented object appears in the Segmented Object List.

1. Move the sliders to adjust the upper and lower intensity thresholds.

The boundary of the new object is updated accordingly.

1. Click in the area of interest again to segment the object according to the selected intensities.

Adjacent (connected) structures in the selected intensity range are added as well.

If the resulting segmented object includes multiple anatomic structures of the heart, you can use the Divide/ Resegment tool to re-segment it into distinct objects.

In addition, you can use the Manual Segmentation tool to add new objects as markers to be used during a CARTOMERGE™ Study:

1. Click the Manual Segmentation button.
2. Hold the **SHIFT** key and click the desired location.

A new object is created as marker at the clicked location and appears in both 2D and 3D views.

Repeat these steps to add more markers.

## CT Acquisition Requirements

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_CT\_acquisition\_v6\_EN.xml

The CARTOSEG™ Segmentation Modules require acquisition of a CTA scan. In order to ensure a high quality scan with minimum radiation exposure to the patient, follow the CTA acquisition guidelines established by the SCCT with respect to physician and technologist competencies, patient screening and preparation, pretest instructions, patient positioning, contrast injection protocols, CTA acquisition, and image reconstruction and post-processing (see ["References"](#target9000003), [1]).

CT data acquisition can be performed on systems from multiple vendors such as Siemens, Philips, and General Electric. A summary of data set acquisition specifications is presented below.

### Data Specifications

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_CT\_dataSpecs\_v6\_EN.xml

It is important to note that the lower the contrast uptake in a particular organ, the lower the accuracy of any automated image segmentation method. For the automatic processing to work at its specified accuracy, the medical image data must comply with the following specifications:

* CTA data
* The CT data acquisition requires the administration of a contrast agent. Native or calcium scoring CTA scans are excluded.
* FOV includes the complete heart; all chambers of the heart must be visible.
* Image contrast uptake must be gated to the objects of interest: 4-chambers, coronaries, coronary sinus, etc.
* Image noise level must be acceptable as specified later in this chapter. For methods to ensure appropriate noise levels, see ["References"](#target9000004), [1].
* The reconstructed and post-processed data must be free of any image reconstruction artifacts. See ["References"](#target9000005), [1] for information on how to avoid artifacts.
* No gaps larger than 1 missing slice.
* No secondary captures.
* Maximum voxel dimensions must not exceed 1 mm.
* Quadratic pixel size (Abs(XPixelSize - YPixelSize) < 0.0189mm).
* Single sample per pixel
* Photometric representation = Monochrome2
* Gantry/Detector tilt <= 1 degree
* No oblique data sets
* Pixel representation 0 or 1

It is important to understand that the presence of high intensity streak artifacts significantly degrades the ability to automatically extract image structures and therefore, there is no guarantee of algorithm accuracy on such data. Also, cases with unusual anatomy and structural heart disease (ventricular hypertrophy, overriding aorta, ventricular septal defect, etc.) cannot be expected to yield a high degree of accuracy.

The data can still be manually processed or edited with the CARTOSEG™ Segmentation Modules.

## MR Acquisition Requirements

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_acquisition\_v6\_EN.xml

### System Specifics

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_dataSpecs\_v6\_EN.xml

#### Field Strength

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_fieldStrength\_v6\_EN.xml

Currently, the majority of clinical MR systems are split between two field strengths: 1.5 T and 3 T. For cardiovascular imaging, most of the research and clinical imaging has been done using 1.5 T field strength. Therefore, the imaging techniques are more standardized and more robust at this field strength. In particular, the Balanced Steady-State Free Precession (bSSFP) MR pulse sequence, the standard for cardiac cine imaging, tends to exhibit more artifacts at 3 T than at 1.5 T. For these reasons, it is recommended to scan at 1.5 T.

#### ECG Signal and Acquisition Prerequisites

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_ecg\_acqPrereq\_v6\_EN.xml

The bulk of cardiovascular MRI protocols rely on a high quality ECG signal to trigger the MR acquisitions. Poor ECG signals lead to poor ECG triggering and negatively affect image quality. In addition, many protocols (including those recommended below) split the data acquisition over multiple heartbeats (often called segmented acquisitions) under the assumption that the heart motion is consistent and periodic. If the subject exhibits significant, consistent arrhythmia, image quality is poor and the only solution is to switch to "real-time" or "single-shot" protocols which do not split data acquisition over multiple heartbeats. The downside to these protocols is that spatial resolution, temporal resolution, and/or contrast-to-noise ratio are worse than in standard, segmented acquisitions. In addition, separate acquisitions may no longer be reliably timed within the cardiac cycle in order to acquire data in the same phase of the cardiac cycle. The recommended protocols listed below assume reliable ECG triggering and switching to real-time or single-shot acquisition strategies may result in the failure of the automatic registration and segmentation.

### MR Protocols

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_protocols\_v6\_EN.xml

The CARTOSEG™ MR Segmentation Module requires two, distinct sets of MR images: anatomical and tissue characterization (TC). The acquisitions for each set are discussed below. These images must be acquired during one MR imaging session and the patient must not be moved or re-landmarked between acquisitions.

#### Anatomical Acquisition

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_anatAcqu\_v6\_EN.xml

The Anatomical Acquisition requires a set of images with the following characteristics:

* 2D cine acquisitions
* Multiple slices in the short-axis orientation covering the mitral valve plane through the apex. Each slice must have the same orientation, slice thickness, FOV, and pixel resolution.
* The 2D cine acquisitions should have more than two phases
* Slices in the long axis orientations are optional and may help the user confirm the results of the automatic registration and segmentation.

For the MR protocol, it is recommended to acquire the ECG-triggered 2D cine images using the bSSFP sequence for optimal contrast and signal. Each slice must be acquired as a segmented acquisition (over multiple heartbeats) and within one breath-holding interval. For the recommended image parameter settings, see the following table.

| Parameter | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Slice Thickness (mm) | <= 8 mm (see ["References"](#target9000006), [2]) |
| Spacing Between Slices (mm) | <= 8 mm |
| Temporal Resolution (ms) | < 45 ms (see ["References"](#target9000007), [2]) |
| Spatial Resolution (mm) | <= 1.8 x 1.8 mm2 |

#### Tissue Characterization Acquisition

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_tissueAcqu\_v6\_EN.xml

The Tissue Characterization Acquisition requires a set of images with the following characteristics:

* 2D acquisitions
* Multiple slices in the short-axis orientation covering the mitral valve plane through the apex. All the slices must have the same orientation, slice thickness, FOV, and pixel resolution.
* The orientation of the TC short-axis slice must match the orientation of the anatomical short-axis slice.
* Slices in the TC long axis orientations are optional and may help the user confirm the results of the automatic registration and segmentation
* The TC series must be acquired after the anatomical 2D cine acquisition

For the MR protocol, the 2D tissue characterization images must be acquired with an ECG-triggered inversion-recovery prepared, spoiled gradient-echo sequence (see [Table 7: "MRI Vendor Acronyms"](#target9000008)) for optimal contrast and signal. Set inversion time to null normal tissue (see ["References"](#target9000009), [2]). Acquire each slice over multiple heartbeats (segmented acquisitions) and within a single breath-holding interval. Segmented data is acquired at every other heartbeat but must be modified to every heartbeat in the setting of bradycardia, and to every third heartbeat in the setting of tachycardia (see ["References"](#target9000010), [2]). The recommended image parameter settings are listed in the following table.

Table 8: TC Acquisition Parameters

| Parameter | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Slice Thickness (mm) | <= 8 mm |
| Spacing between Slices (mm) | <= 8 mm |
| Temporal Resolution (ms) | < 45 ms (see ["References"](#target9000011), [2]) |
| Spatial Resolution (mm) | <= 1.8 × 1.8 mm2 (see ["References"](#target9000012), [2]) |

#### 3D MR Volume Acquisition

https://bwiildita.sharepoint.com/sites/prod/Topics/cartoSeg\_mr\_3DVolAcqu\_v6\_EN.xml

The CARTOSEG™ MR Segmentation Module 3D MR provides the ability to manipulate arbitrary 3D MR series that can be formed into a consistent 3D volume. The user can manipulate non-standard acquisitions, even though the automatic and semi-automatic segmentation tools are not available. The acquired volumes must still satisfy the DICOM series requirements.
